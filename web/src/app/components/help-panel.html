How this works

Splitting costs between friends is rarely an even divide: one person books the flights, another covers dinner, and nobody ate the same number of pizza slices. This app records what was bought and who benefited, then works out the smallest set of payments that makes everyone square.

The five steps

All but the last happen in one grid, on the Split tab.

  1. People — everyone taking part is a column. + Add person, above the grid, makes one; type the name in its header.
  2. Expense sheets — one per check or category, each spanning its own block of rows. + at the top of the grid's first column adds one.
  3. Lines — type on the blank row at the end of a sheet's block to add one, with its amount. Lines are numbered from one within their own sheet by default — see Display settings below to number them across the whole trip instead. Drag a line by its number to reorder it within its sheet. Tick the box at the start of any line to remove it, give everyone a share of it, or clear its shares — the buttons appear above the grid, and act on every line you have ticked.
  4. Sheet settings — the sheet's own cell holds its name and its tax, tip and discount: type straight into them. Each takes either an amount (5.61) or a percentage (15%). ⋯ in that cell opens the rest — currency, exchange rate, who paid, and deleting the sheet.
  5. Settle up — expand a split's row on the Splits tab to read off who pays whom.

Share ratios

Share values are relative, not amounts. Within a row they only mean something compared to each other, so the app never asks you to do arithmetic.

  • 1 1 1 — three people split a line evenly.
  • 2 2 — also an even split. The ratio is what counts, not the size.
  • 1 3 4 — an 8-slice pizza where one had 1 slice, another 3, the last 4.
  • Leave a cell empty for anyone who did not benefit from that line.

Tax, tip and discount are spread across the lines of their sheet in proportion to price, then follow the same share ratios.

Recording who paid

There are two ways, and you would normally use whichever fits the receipt:

  • Per sheet — open ⋯ on the sheet's cell on the Split tab and tick people under Paid by. They cover that whole sheet, split equally between them. This is the usual case: one person hands over a card for the whole check.
  • Per line — use the decimal part of a split cell. 1.2 means "owes one share, and already paid two shares' worth". That covers a camping trip where everyone brought something different.
The two cannot be mixed on the same sheet — if a sheet declares payers, its per-line payments are ignored and the app says so. And once anyone is recorded as paying, every priced line needs a payer, otherwise the balances silently understate what is owed.

Reading the balances

The row above the names is what each person's card should be charged. A figure in parentheses and red means the opposite: that person is owed money.

  • When nobody is marked as paying, the balances simply add up to the trip total — this is the "split the check" case.
  • When payers are recorded, the balances net to zero: every debt has a matching credit.

Settlement groups

Usually the simplest way to settle is for everyone to pay whoever is owed the most, and for that person to pay the rest. Sometimes the balances break into independent subsets that can each settle among themselves, which needs fewer payments. When that happens the app colours each group with a dot and lists the payments separately.

The odd cent

Rounding each person's balance to the nearest cent can leave the column a cent or two off the total. The app nudges as many people as needed by ±0.01 so the figures always reconcile exactly.

Different currencies

Each expense sheet can keep its own currency while the split happens in the trip's base currency. Both currency boxes can be typed into — “forint”, “huf” or “hungarian” all find the same one. Pick a currency in the sheet's editor and a rate appears:

  • Leave it alone to use the built-in rate.
  • Type your own to pin it — useful when you know what the card actually charged.
  • Clear the box to go back to the built-in rate.

Rates are a fixed snapshot bundled with the app, not a live feed.

Selecting, copying and filling cells

Item, Amount and each person's share work like cells in a spreadsheet, not just boxes typed into one at a time.

  • Selecting a block — click a cell to select it, then drag, or shift-click another cell, to grow the selection into a rectangle. The arrow keys move the selection one cell at a time; hold Shift to grow it in that direction instead of moving it.
  • Copy and paste — Ctrl+C copies the selection as text any spreadsheet can read. Ctrl+V pastes it back: with a single cell selected, the paste starts there and grows to fit whatever is on the clipboard; with a block selected, the clipboard is repeated to fill every cell in it — handy for putting the same share on several lines at once.
  • The fill handle — the small square at a selection's own bottom-right corner. Drag it to repeat the selection into whichever cells it is dragged over, the same way a spreadsheet's own fill handle does.

All three stay within a sheet's real lines — the line number, the Sheet cell, and the blank row that starts a new one are never part of a selection.

Copying, cutting and pasting whole lines

Tick one or more lines — the same tick used for Everyone / Clear shares / Remove — and the toolbar's Copy, Cut and Paste buttons act on them as whole lines: name, amount and every person's share together, not just the values a cell copy carries.

  • Copy (or Ctrl+C with lines ticked) snapshots the ticked lines and un-ticks them, marking them with a dashed outline instead — a spreadsheet's own "marching ants," for what a Paste will place. Ready to paste as many times as you like; the outline stays until a fresh Copy or Cut replaces it, or Esc dismisses it.
  • Cut (Ctrl+X) snapshots them too, but does not remove them yet — the way Explorer's own Cut works, not a spreadsheet's. The lines dim, in place, until either Paste actually moves them, or Esc cancels the cut and brings them back to normal. A cut can only be pasted once — the source lines are removed the moment it lands, so a second Paste has nothing left to place.
  • Paste (or Ctrl+V with lines ticked) inserts the lines right after the last ticked line — or, with nothing ticked, after whichever cell is currently selected — or, on a sheet with no lines yet, after clicking its own + Add item row, which has no cell of its own to select. This works across sheets: shares belong to the trip, not to any one sheet, so a person's share carries over even when the lines land somewhere else.

Ctrl+C/Ctrl+X/Ctrl+V only act on lines when some are ticked — otherwise they are the cell copy/paste described above. The toolbar's own Paste button is more flexible: it also works off a plain cell selection or an empty sheet's own add-item row, with nothing ticked.

Undo and redo

Undo and Redo, in the Split tab's toolbar (or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y), step back and forward through changes to the split that's open — typing a line, removing a sheet or a person, pasting or dragging a block of cells, all of it. Typing into the same box counts as one step until you move on to something else, so undoing a rename takes back the whole name, not one keystroke; a paste or a bulk removal is one step too, whatever it touched. History is per split and lives only in this tab, so switching splits or reloading the page starts it fresh.

Reorder sheets

Reorder sheets, in the Split tab's toolbar, opens a dialog listing every expense sheet in the trip — for a split with more of them than are easy to drag into place by eye.

  • Click a sheet to select it. Click more sheets to add them to the selection, or click a selected one again to remove it. The arrow keys then move the whole selection up or down together, keeping their order.
  • Or skip selecting and just drag a sheet by its handle.

Display settings

Settings, in the Split tab's toolbar, holds a couple of preferences that apply to every split, not just the one open — they are not part of any split's own data, so they are never included in an export.

  • Totals band height — stretches the balances row above the grid, for when a name or a figure needs more room to breathe. Leave it blank for the default.
  • Highlight row on hover — turns the shaded line that follows your cursor down the grid on or off.
  • Number lines across all sheets — off by default, so each sheet's lines number from one on their own. Turn it on to have the numbers count up across the whole trip instead, continuing where the sheet before left off.

Keeping several splits

The Splits tab lists everything you have saved, most recently edited first — a trip, last week's dinner, the flat's bills. Open one to work on it; the others are left exactly as they were.

  • New split starts an empty one.
  • Duplicate copies a split, which is handy when the same people split costs the same way every month.
  • Delete removes one for good. Deleting your last split leaves a fresh empty one, so there is always something to work on.
  • Settle up, at the bottom of a row, expands it to show who pays whom for that split — no need to open it first.

Once the list grows, the search box narrows it. It looks at more than the name — the people, the expense sheets and the items are all searchable, so “sarah”, “peruvian” or “plane tickets” each find the right split even when it is still called New Split. Several words narrow further, and each result says what it matched.

Sort by reorders the list: by when you last worked on a split, when you added it, its name, or its total. The choice is remembered, and applies to search results too.

Where the data lives

In this browser, on this device. Splits are saved as you type, so closing the tab or reloading picks up where you left off. Nothing is sent anywhere — there is no account and no server.

Two consequences worth knowing:

  • Another device, another browser, or a private window will not see these splits. There is no sharing yet.
  • Clearing your browsing data clears them too.

If you have the app open in more than one tab, they stay in step, and each tab remembers which split it was showing — you can keep two splits open side by side. Should you happen to be editing the same split in two tabs at once, the app asks which version to keep rather than picking for you.

Moving splits somewhere else

Export downloads a split as a JSON file: people, every expense sheet, every share. Export all on the Splits tab puts the whole library in one file. Either is the way to keep a backup, move splits to another device, or send one to somebody who can open it with Import.

Importing adds to your splits — it never overwrites what is already saved. A file imported twice gives you two independent copies.
